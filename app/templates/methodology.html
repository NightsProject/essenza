Methodology

Study design

This study asks whether fine-tuning improves a pretrained Transformer's ability to produce reference-like summaries while respecting maximum length limits of 10%, 20%, and 30% of the source.

Baseline
Original google/flan-t5-small, without project-specific fine-tuning.
Fine-tuned model
The same base checkpoint after fine-tuning on compression-conditioned examples.
Evaluation data
The later experiments use 540 training sources, 110 validation sources, and 50 held-out test sources. Each source has reference summaries for all three compression conditions.

The primary comparison is pretrained baseline versus ordinary fine-tuned generation. Auxiliary-guided inference is excluded.

Compression conditions

The selected percentage is a maximum word-count cap relative to the source, not a target that summaries must fill:

  • 10% — extreme compression
  • 20% — high compression (default)
  • 30% — moderate compression

Human reference summaries average approximately 7.86%, 16.86%, and 26.05% of source length for the corresponding 10%, 20%, and 30% conditions.

Webapp generation setup

These settings describe interactive webapp generation; they should not be confused with every training/evaluation notebook configuration.

Input limit
512 tokens. Inputs exceeding this limit are rejected with a clear error.
Output limit
128 generated tokens.
Decoding
Deterministic beam search: num_beams=4, do_sample=False.
Device
CUDA when available; otherwise CPU.

Evaluation measures

  • Actual compression: generated summary word count divided by source word count, multiplied by 100. Word counts are whitespace-delimited.
  • Strict cap compliance: percentage of outputs whose actual compression is at or below the selected maximum.
  • ROUGE-L F1: overlap-based similarity between generated summaries and human references. This is an automatic similarity measure, not a direct factuality score.
  • Paired bootstrap: confidence intervals for fine-tuned-minus-baseline ROUGE-L differences, paired by test source. The test source is the resampling unit, keeping its compression conditions together. An interval excluding zero supports a non-zero difference for that run; it does not establish which fine-tuned checkpoint is best.

Latest documented comparison: Experiment 14

On 50 held-out test sources, baseline versus fine-tuned ROUGE-L F1 was 0.208 versus 0.391 at 10%, 0.251 versus 0.358 at 20%, and 0.252 versus 0.390 at 30%. The paired fine-tuned-minus-baseline differences were +0.1826 (95% CI 0.1262–0.2371), +0.1064 (0.0739–0.1382), and +0.1383 (0.1041–0.1714); all three intervals exclude zero.

Strict cap compliance for the fine-tuned model was 94%, 78%, and 58% at 10%, 20%, and 30%, compared with 0%, 26%, and 54% for the baseline. The 30% compliance difference was not statistically significant in the reported paired bootstrap and remains a weakness.

Interpretation and limitations

  • Experiments vary in dataset splits, prompts, training settings, and sometimes test-set size. Cross-experiment rankings are descriptive unless checkpoints are evaluated on identical sources using the same protocol and a direct paired test.
  • Compliance alone can reward summaries that are much shorter than the requested cap. Interpret compliance alongside actual compression and ROUGE-L.
  • ROUGE-L and length metrics do not prove factual consistency, complete fact coverage, or usefulness; these require separate human assessment.
  • The webapp supports interactive generation and side-by-side comparison. It does not reproduce the full held-out research evaluation or guarantee factual correctness.
